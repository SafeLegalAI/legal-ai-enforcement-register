 1                                           JURISDICTION

 2   A.        This Court Has Subject Matter Jurisdiction Under the Class Action Fairness Act

 3             4.    Under the Class Action Fairness Act (“CAFA”) this Court has original jurisdiction

 4   over class actions involving 100 or more putative class members, minimal diversity, and where

 5   the amount in controversy exceeds $5 million dollars. See 28 U.S.C. § 1332(d)(2), (d)(5). Should

 6   these requirements be satisfied, removal is warranted pursuant to 28 U.S.C. §§ 1446, 1453(b).

 7                    i.    CAFA’s Numerosity Requirement is Satisfied as There Are 100 Or

 8                          More Putative Class Members

 9             5.    The Complaint alleges that the class members are “[a]ll residents of the State of

10   California who purchased subscriptions to DoNotPay.com,” (“Class”). Compl. ¶ 36. The

11   Complaint also states that “on information and belief, there are thousands of people in the

12   Class[.]” Compl. ¶ 37. Plaintiff’s own assertions regarding the number of class plaintiffs are

13   sufficient to establish that the numerosity requirement is satisfied. See Nolan v. Kayo Oil Co.,

14   No. C 11-00707 MEJ, 2011 WL 2650973, at *2 (N.D. Cal. July 6, 2011).

15                   ii.    CAFA’s “Minimal” Diversity Requirement is Satisfied

16             6.    Under CAFA, parties are considered diverse if “any member of a class of plaintiffs

17   is a citizen of a State different from any defendant[.]” 28 U.S.C. § 1332(d)(2)(A) (emphasis

18   added).

19             7.    As a corporation, DoNotPay is “deemed to be a citizen of every State and foreign

20   state by which it has been incorporated and of the State or foreign state where it has its principal

21   place of business[.]” 28 U.S.C. § 1332(c)(1). A corporation’s principal place of business is where

22   “a corporation’s officers direct, control, and coordinate the corporation’s activities.” See Hertz

23   Corp. v. Friend, 559 U.S. 77, 92-93 (2010).

24             8.    DoNotPay is a Delaware corporation with its principal place of business at 691

25   Pfister Drive, Aspen, Colorado 81611. Although the Complaint asserts that DoNotPay’s

26   “principal place of business [is] in San Francisco, California,” (Compl. ¶ 9), it is the party’s

27   “actual citizenship [that] controls—not the plaintiff's mistaken allegations.” Strotek Corp. v. Air

11                  (iii)   The Amount in Controversy Exceeds $5,000,000

12          10.     While Plaintiff does not specify an amount of damages in his Complaint, he seeks

13   restitution “of all amounts Plaintiff and the Class”—which includes “[a]ll residents of the State

14   of California who purchased subscriptions to DoNotPay.com,” an amount Plaintiff deems to be

15   in the “thousands”—paid to Defendant, injunctive and equitable relief, litigation expenses, and

16   attorney’s fees. Compl. ¶¶ 36-37, Prayer for Relief.

17          11.     When it is “unclear or ambiguous from the face of a state-court complaint whether

18   the requisite amount in controversy is pled,” courts apply a “preponderance of the evidence”

19   standard—meaning it must be “more likely than not” that the amount in controversy exceeds $5

20   million. See Guglielmino v. McKee Foods Corp., 506 F.3d 696, 699 (9th Cir. 2007) (citation

21   omitted).

22          12.     “In any class action, the claims of the individual class members shall be

23   aggregated to determine whether the matter in controversy exceeds the sum or value of

24   $5,000,000, exclusive of interest and costs.” 28 U.S.C. § 1332(d)(6). Thus, courts can consider
